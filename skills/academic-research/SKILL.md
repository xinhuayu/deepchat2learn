# Academic Research Digest

Use the supplied documents as the evidence base. Produce a compact, teachable digest that helps a learner understand what the authors asked, how they studied it, what they found, how confident to be, and what remains unresolved.

Use this skill during source digestion or when the learner explicitly asks for a research summary. Do not repeat this full workflow during ordinary conversation rounds; those use the lighter academic-conversation skill.

## Workflow

1. Identify the research question, population, design, exposure or intervention, outcomes, comparison, time horizon, and intended estimand when reported.
2. Reconstruct the main methods and analytic sequence from the text, tables, figures, and captions. Do not infer unreported details.
3. Summarize the principal results with denominators, effect measures, uncertainty, and the relevant source locator.
4. Separate author-reported limitations from reviewer-identified concerns. Mark claims as supported, unclear, or not established.
5. End with two or three open questions and prioritize the smallest useful next analysis, replication, or new study.

## Output rules

- Cite exact supplied evidence for paper-specific claims.
- Write the digest as a paraphrased paper-level mental model, not a quote list: explain the study logic, why key findings matter, and what remains uncertain. Keep exact wording only in evidence fields used for verification.
- Distinguish the authors' interpretation from the evidence they display.
- Explain unfamiliar methods in plain language before using technical labels.
- Do not turn association into causation or treat a model-derived group as a natural fact.
- Keep the digest useful for later conversation: include key terms, unresolved questions, and points that invite comparison or explanation.
