# Epidemiologic Methods Critique

Produce a methods-centered critique linking the question, target, design, assumptions, analysis, evidence, and supported inference. Use substantive context only to judge importance, validity, or applicability.

Use this skill for an explicit epidemiology methods review or a source-digestion pass selected for epidemiology. Do not apply the full critique workflow to every conversation follow-up; ordinary turns use the compact academic-conversation skill.

For a full paper review, read [references/critique-guideline.md](references/critique-guideline.md). For a focused request, read the relevant section.

## Process

1. **Frame:** Classify the aim as descriptive, predictive, associational, or causal. Assess its justification and define the target population, outcome, time scale, and estimand.
2. **Reconstruct:** Identify the data source, cohort construction, variables, follow-up, missingness, and exact analytic sequence. Trace each principal estimate to its model and analytic sample.
3. **Evaluate:** Assess design fit, measurement, selection, confounding, time alignment, modeling assumptions, transportability, and reproducibility.
4. **Verify:** Audit material tables and figures; compare displayed evidence with the text, discussion, prior evidence, and authors' interpretation.
5. **Prioritize:** Rank concerns by likely impact. Present major concerns as **target -> assumption -> evidence -> consequence -> diagnostic -> remedy**. Recommend the smallest informative reanalysis or study.

For later conversation, produce a compact digest that paraphrases the paper's epidemiologic logic and separates reported evidence, interpretation, uncertainty, and additional context. Do not turn the digest into a list of copied passages; reserve exact excerpts for evidence verification.

## Report

1. Question justification, design, population, and estimand
2. Exposure, outcome, and analysis reconstruction
3. Main results and table/figure verification
4. Strengths and major validity concerns
5. Diagnostics, sensitivity analyses, and defensible alternatives
6. Supported inference, overall judgment, and future priorities

## Standards

- Distinguish **not reported**, **unclear**, **at risk**, and **incorrect**.
- Separate identification assumptions, statistical assumptions, and data limitations.
- Distinguish confounders, mediators, colliders, competing events, and post-exposure variables.
- Do not infer causality from temporality, adjustment, propensity scores, or significance.
- Match every proposed method to a target, data structure, and assumptions.
- Separate prespecified from exploratory analyses and statistical from epidemiologic importance.
- State when evidence is insufficient or a method is outside the reviewer's expertise.

Conclude with what is supported, what remains uncertain or unidentified, and the highest-value next step.
